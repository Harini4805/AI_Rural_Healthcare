A Product Requirements Document (PRD) is the foundational strategic blueprint for any product
initiative, especially innovative ones. It articulates the crucial why, what, and for whom of the product,
aligning diverse teams towards a common goal. A well-crafted PRD specifies product functionalities, features,
and success criteria, ensuring the final product meets customer needs and drives business growth. For
innovative products, it is particularly important to capture the dynamic, iterative nature of development, which
can be challenging with traditional text-heavy documents. Mind maps are presented as indispensable
visual tools for injecting clarity, fostering shared understanding, visualizing complex relationships,
and managing risks associated with innovation directly within the PRD framework.
Here is a blueprint template for a PRD, drawing on the essential mind maps and information from the sources:
Product Requirements Document (PRD) Blueprint
I. Introduction
• 1.1 Purpose: This PRD defines the AI Rural Healthcare Dashboard, a predictive intelligence platform
designed to transform reactive rural healthcare into proactive disease prevention through explainable AI,
real-time risk prioritization, and optimized resource allocation.
• 1.2 Goals and Objectives: Enable district administrators to reduce outbreak response times by 90%,
decrease preventable mortality by 25%, improve resource efficiency by 35%, and build a self-improving
health intelligence network through continuous field feedback integration.
• 1.3 Scope: The platform covers district-level health administration, operational coordination, and field
worker interfaces with features including village risk scoring, what-if simulation, resource allocation tools,
mobile-responsive alerts, and bidirectional feedback mechanisms for rural healthcare systems in
developing nations.
• 1.4 Target Audience (for the PRD): District health administrators managing strategic planning and
resource allocation, health coordinators overseeing operational deployment across village clusters, and
field health workers delivering frontline care and collecting ground-truth observations.
II. Product Vision and Strategy
• 2.1 Product Vision: Create a self-improving health intelligence network where every district contributes to
regional disease prevention patterns, every intervention refines predictive accuracy, and progressively
healthier rural communities emerge through transparent, data-driven, collaborative preventive care.
• 2.2 Strategic Objectives: Establish the platform as the standard for rural health intelligence in developing
nations by demonstrating measurable mortality reduction, building trust through explainable AI, enabling
evidence-based policy decisions through what-if simulation, and creating sustainable improvement cycles
through field feedback integration.
• 2.3 Core Value Proposition: Transform invisible health risks into actionable intelligence by providing
real-time village prioritization, transparent AI explanations that build trust, scenario simulation for strategic
planning, and bidirectional feedback that continuously improves prediction accuracy.
• 2.4 Key Differentiators: Unlike reactive reporting systems, this platform predicts outbreaks before they
occur with explainable reasoning, simulates intervention impacts before resource commitment, learns from
field observations to improve accuracy, and provides role-appropriate interfaces from strategic dashboards
to mobile alerts.
III. Target Audience and User Understanding
• 3.1.1 User Persona: District Health Administrator:
Goal: Allocate limited resources strategically to prevent disease outbreaks and reduce mortality across the
district while demonstrating impact to secure future funding.
Journey: Select district to view autofiltered priority list of villages ranked by AIpredicted risk severity, Use
whatif simulator to model intervention scenarios and project impact on risk coverage and lives saved,
Review explainable AI breakdowns to understand risk drivers and make evidencebased resource allocation
decisions
• 3.1.2 User Persona: Health Coordinator:
Goal: Deploy mobile units and manage medicine inventory efficiently across assigned village clusters to
maximize outbreak prevention within operational constraints.
Journey: View operational dashboard showing assigned cluster priorities with deployment
recommendations, Monitor realtime inventory levels and staff availability across cluster facilities,
Coordinate mobile unit schedules based on risk updates and field worker feedback
• 3.1.3 User Persona: Field Health Worker:
Goal: Deliver targeted preventive care to high-risk villages and contribute ground-truth observations that
improve system accuracy.
Journey: Receive mobile alerts showing villagespecific risk levels with clear intervention checklists,
Complete preventive care activities guided by explainable risk factors, Submit feedback on actual
conditions versus predictions to refine future AI accuracy
• 3.2 User Needs and Expectations: Administrators need strategic visibility and simulation tools for
resource decisions, coordinators require operational clarity for deployment efficiency, and field workers
expect clear priorities with understandable rationale plus mechanisms to contribute their expertise back into
the system.
• 3.3 Context of Use: The platform operates in resource-constrained rural settings with intermittent
connectivity, requiring mobile-responsive interfaces for field workers, desktop dashboards for
administrators, role-based access controls for data security, and offline-capable feedback submission with
sync-when-connected functionality.
IV. User Journey and Experience
• 4.1 Key User Scenarios: Administrator simulates deploying two mobile units to top-risk cluster and
receives projected impact on district mortality; coordinator receives alert about medicine shortage in
high-risk village and reallocates from low-risk facility; field worker logs unexpected disease cluster that
wasn't predicted, triggering AI model refinement.
• 4.2 User Journey Maps: Administrator journey flows from district selection through priority review to
what-if simulation and resource commitment; coordinator journey spans cluster monitoring through
deployment coordination to inventory management; field worker journey progresses from alert reception
through intervention delivery to feedback submission.
• 4.3 Pain Points and Opportunities: Current pain points include invisible outbreak risks, resource
misallocation, delayed responses, and distrust of black-box predictions; opportunities include explainable
AI building trust, what-if simulation enabling evidence-based advocacy, field feedback creating continuous
improvement, and role-appropriate interfaces maximizing adoption.
V. Product Features and Functionality
• 5.1 Feature Breakdown: Core features include district selection with auto-filtered village priority lists,
explainable AI risk scoring showing percentage contribution of each factor, what-if simulator for intervention
scenario modeling, bidirectional feedback loop for field observations, role-based dashboards with
appropriate access levels, mobile-responsive interfaces for field workers, real-time inventory tracking, and
automated alert generation for emerging risks.
• 5.2 Functional Requirements: System must synthesize historical disease patterns, seasonal trends,
infrastructure data, staffing levels, and inventory to generate village risk scores; update priorities in
real-time as new data arrives; provide explainable breakdowns for each score; simulate intervention
scenarios with projected impact metrics; accept and integrate field worker feedback; support role-based
authentication; function on mobile devices with intermittent connectivity; and generate exportable reports
for policy advocacy.
• 5.3 Non-Functional Requirements: Platform must maintain 99.5% uptime during critical periods, process
risk updates within 5 seconds, support 1000+ concurrent users per district, ensure data privacy compliance
with health regulations, provide mobile interfaces functional on 3G networks, maintain prediction accuracy
above 80%, scale to national deployment across 100+ districts, and support offline feedback submission
with automatic sync.
VI. Market Analysis and Competitive Landscape
• 6.1 Market Opportunity: Rural healthcare in developing nations represents a $50B+ market with 2 billion
people underserved by preventive intelligence; government health budgets increasingly prioritize digital
transformation; donor organizations seek measurable impact platforms; and successful pilots
demonstrating 28% mortality reduction create compelling adoption drivers.
• 6.2 Competitive Analysis: Traditional health information systems provide retrospective reporting without
prediction; commercial analytics platforms lack rural-specific features and affordability; academic research
tools don't scale to operational deployment; this platform uniquely combines predictive AI, explainability,
what-if simulation, field feedback integration, and role-appropriate interfaces at accessible price points for
developing nation budgets.
